Single-Cell RNA Sequencing (scRNA-seq) resolves the issues of data averaging and off-target noise associated with traditional bulk RNA-seq in several ways:

1. **Individual cell representation**: scRNA-seq provides a snapshot of the transcriptome for each individual cell, allowing for precise detection of rare or aberrant cells like T-cells or Cancer-Associated Fibroblasts.
2. **Cell-type specificity**: By analyzing the RNA profiles of purified immune and tumor cells, researchers can determine which gene pairs are specifically expressed in cancerous or immune cells, eliminating off-target effects.
3. **Reduced statistical noise**: scRNA-seq datasets typically contain tens of thousands to millions of individual cells, greatly reducing the impact of averaging outliers and noise from rare populations.

This leads to a more accurate identification of tumor-specific biosensor gene pairs, as scRNA-seq can detect subtle changes in expression that may not be observable in bulk RNA-seq data. By analyzing scRNA-seq data, researchers can precisely characterize immune responses in cancer and identify novel targets for therapy.
