OCLAD2 (Oncogene-induced cell adhesion molecule 2) and CEACAM5 (Carbonic anhydrase V) are epithelial-specific genes that play crucial roles in maintaining the integrity of the epithelial barrier in glandular/epithelial tumors. OCLAD2 is involved in regulating cell-cell adhesion, promoting epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and suppressing tumor metastasis. CEACAM5, a transmembrane glycoprotein, facilitates cell signaling mediated by various growth factors and cytokines, modulating cell proliferation, migration, and tumor angiogenesis.

The high co-expression of OCLAD2 and CEACAM5 in malignant ductal/epithelial cells, such as pancreatic cancer, is due to their overlapping roles in promoting epithelial polarity, stemness, and metastatic potential. They act together to establish a network that sustains the tumorigenic phenotype.

The circularity data leakage issue with CEACAM5 in pancreatic cancer annotations can be attributed to its ubiquitous expression across different cell types, including normal epithelial cells and stromal components. This widespread expression leads to "artificial" correlations between CEACAM5 levels and tumor markers, causing data leakage. Moreover, the relatively small size of the protein and its structural homology with other members of the CEACAM family may obscure subtle differences in expression patterns among cancer types, further exacerbating the problem.
